Safe Play and Enrichment Ideas for Your Rabbit

Practical guide

Rabbits enjoy moving, exploring and hiding. Enrichment need not be elaborate or expensive: a suitable space and attentive supervision matter most, because what works for one rabbit may not suit another.

Start with room to move

Provide a secure area where your rabbit can stretch and move, with a non-slip surface and somewhere to hide. Remove accessible cables, unsafe plants and small objects; supervise exploration in a new space. Keep clean water and suitable food available.

Try a simple tunnel or hide

A clean, uncoated cardboard box with two roomy openings can become a hide after tape, staples, string and other swallowable fasteners are removed. Make sure it is stable and let your rabbit enter and leave freely rather than putting them inside.

Watch how toys are used

Your rabbit may enjoy exploring or shredding cardboard. Supervise play, remove the box if they eat a lot of it and ask a veterinarian if concerned. Choose rabbit-appropriate items and avoid small parts, sharp edges and string.

Make room for choice and rest

Place the hide somewhere quiet and do not chase your rabbit into using a toy. Sit nearby and let them approach, with an adult supervising children. If a toy attracts no interest, remove it and try a different suitable activity without pressure.

Eating comes first

Play does not replace a rabbit’s basic needs. Keep clean water and suitable hay available and ask a rabbit-experienced veterinarian about diet. If your rabbit stops eating or misses a meal, contact a veterinarian immediately; do not wait for play to solve the problem.

Frequently asked question

Is every cardboard box suitable for a rabbit?

No. Avoid coated or dirty boxes and remove tape, staples and string. Supervise your rabbit and take the cardboard away if they eat a lot of it.
