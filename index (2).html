How to Prepare Your Home for a New Cat

Practical guide

A new home can be full of unfamiliar sounds and smells. Your cat does not need to explore everything on the first day: a calm start gives them room to become familiar with their surroundings at their own pace.

Start with a quiet room

Choose a secure room away from busy household activity. Provide a comfortable sleeping place and a hiding spot, such as a stable box. Bring the carrier into the room, open the door and let your cat come out when ready. Do not pull them out or force interaction.

Arrange food, water and the litter tray

Offer clean water and suitable food in accessible places. Keep the litter tray in a quiet spot away from food and water, and clean it regularly. Ask the previous caregiver what food and litter your cat is used to before making sudden changes.

Make exploring safer

Secure windows and balconies, and remove accessible cords, string and small objects that could be swallowed. Check whether household plants are safe before placing them within reach. Use suitable toys, supervise string toys and put them away after play.

Get to know each other gently

Sit nearby, speak softly and let your cat approach you. Do not force cuddles or play; teach children to leave the cat alone while sleeping or hiding. If another pet lives at home, begin with separate spaces and introduce them gradually under supervision.

When to seek help

Cats settle at different rates, but do not assume every change is caused by the move. If your cat stops eating, has difficulty breathing or shows a concerning change, contact a veterinarian promptly.

Frequently asked question

Should my cat explore the whole home immediately?

No. Start with a safe space and allow time to settle, then introduce other rooms gradually when your cat seems ready.
