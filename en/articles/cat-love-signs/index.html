10 Signs Your Cat Loves You (No. 8 Is Misunderstood by Almost Everyone)

✍️ The Anis Team📅 30 October 2026⏱ 4 min read🔄 Last updated: 30 October 2026

People say cats are aloof… the truth is they love differently: quietly, in code, and it takes a trained eye. Here is your complete dictionary of feline love.

1) The slow blink

When your cat looks at you and slowly closes then opens her eyes, that is a "cat kiss": a signal of complete safety and trust. Try blinking back the same way — her response will amaze you.

2) Head bunting

Facial glands secrete identifying pheromones; when she rubs her head against you she is leaving her "signature": you are mine, and I am safe with you.

3) Kneading

A clawing motion inherited from nursing; doing it on your lap means she has returned to her earliest sense of safety.

4) The upright tail with a hooked tip

An unmistakable feline greeting, the equivalent of "hello, I'm happy to see you!". A tail quivering near you is affection one notch higher.

5) Sleeping beside you — or on you

Cats only sleep where they feel protected; choosing you as a nap spot is a badge of trust.

6) Following you from room to room

It isn't police surveillance — it's a desire for closeness and social company.

7) Gifts… yes, gifts

Dragging you a toy mouse, or even real prey, is maternal teaching behaviour: "I feed you and I teach you". Accept the gift graciously; never punish it.

8) The exposed belly — the most common misreading!

A bared belly is enormous trust, but for most cats it is not an invitation to pet; touching there can spring the "claw trap". Looking and smiling is the correct reply.

9) Purring when you approach

A greeting purr differs from a pain purr; if it starts the moment she sees you, it is a pure song of affection.

10) The gentle lick

Allogrooming is cat-to-cat behaviour; bestowing it on you means you've been admitted to the family.

How to return the love in her language?

  • Exchange slow blinks and avoid long, direct staring.
  • Pet the head and cheeks (gland zones) instead of the belly.
  • Short daily play sessions that mimic hunting.
  • Respect her refusal signals: a thumping tail or flattened ears means stop immediately.
