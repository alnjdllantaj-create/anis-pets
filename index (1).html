How to Make Your Home Safer for a Pet Bird

Practical guide

A curious bird experiences your home as part of its environment. Simple changes can reduce hazards, but bird species have different needs, so ask a bird-experienced veterinarian about your companion’s particular care.

Choose a suitable place

Place the cage somewhere stable and calm, away from the kitchen and excessive noise, with suitable ventilation but no direct draught. Make sure food and water are accessible and clean the bowls and living area regularly.

Pay attention to air and fumes

Keep your bird away from cigarette smoke, sprays, air fresheners and cleaning fumes. Some non-stick PTFE-coated cookware and appliances can release toxic fumes when heated, and fumes can travel between rooms; avoiding these products in a home with birds is recommended.

Check toys and nearby objects

Choose toys appropriate to the bird’s size and inspect them regularly. Avoid swallowable small parts, loose threads and sharp edges, and remove damaged toys. Keep cords, plants and medicines out of reach.

Make out-of-cage time safer

If your bird comes out of the cage, supervise them, close doors and windows and switch off fans. Watch out for glass, mirrors and open water containers. Never leave the bird alone with cats, dogs or other animals.

When health is a concern

If your bird is exposed to smoke or fumes, has difficulty breathing or shows an obvious concerning change, contact a bird-experienced veterinarian immediately. Do not wait a fixed period or substitute home treatment for advice.

Frequently asked question

Is placing the cage away from the kitchen enough to prevent all fume exposure?

No. Some fumes can travel between rooms. Avoid the source in the home and ask a qualified veterinarian about products whose safety is uncertain.
